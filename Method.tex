\begin{abstract}
In edge intelligence, large volumes of data from different modalities, such as images and mobile-sensor time series, are continuously generated by clients. These data remain distributed due to deployment constraints, making Federated Learning (FL) a natural paradigm for collaborative training without centralizing raw data. However, conventional FL relies on substantial on-device training and uniform aggregation, facing challenges from constrained resources, scarce labels, and heterogeneous environments. To address these challenges, we propose Communication-Efficient FL with Quality-Aware Graph Aggregation (EdgeQGFed), a cloud-edge-device collaborative framework for edge intelligence across data modalities. EdgeQGFed shifts most training to edge servers, keeps clients lightweight, and enables cloud-coordinated cross-edge knowledge sharing beyond simple averaging. At the edge, it couples cloud-edge consistency with confidence-weighted pseudo-labeling to exploit unlabeled data and suppress unreliable predictions. At the cloud, a quality-aware edge graph models inter-edge relationships and update reliability from model, prototype, and training-quality signals. With graph attention, EdgeQGFed routes cross-edge knowledge selectively for personalized updates, mitigating negative transfer from non-independent and identically distributed (non-IID) or low-quality updates. Experiments on image, mobile-sensor, and network-traffic tasks show up to a 6.32\% performance improvement and 1.48$\times$ higher communication efficiency over state-of-the-art heterogeneous semi-supervised FL frameworks. Code is available at https://anonymous.4open.science/r/EdgeQGFed-0606.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Large models have accelerated the growth of edge intelligence tasks~\cite{wang2025empowering}. Many applications require model updates and decision-making close to data sources to reduce cloud communication pressure, protect privacy, and improve response efficiency~\cite{kumar2026task, Min2026Task}. Unlike centralized intelligence, edge data are distributed across many terminal devices and include both image data and mobile-sensor time series, showing distinct cross-modal characteristics. However, terminal devices are constrained by computation, energy, bandwidth, and connection stability, making complex training difficult. Uploading raw data to the cloud would also incur high communication overhead and privacy risks~\cite{jia2025comprehensive}. Therefore, efficient and stable collaborative learning in resource-constrained edge networks with diverse data modalities is a key problem in edge intelligence systems.

Federated Learning (FL) provides a feasible paradigm for distributed training. However, traditional FL usually adopts a two-tier ``cloud-client'' architecture and assumes that clients can train locally and periodically upload model updates~\cite{zhang2026source}. This assumption does not always hold in real edge networks. Weak clients can hardly afford complex models, while link heterogeneity and mobile dropouts increase latency and communication cost. Direct cloud access by many terminal devices further amplifies uplink pressure and synchronization waiting time, making the system difficult to scale. Meanwhile, edge data are often not independent and identically distributed (non-IID) and have limited labels~\cite{Xu2026SemiSFL}. In contrast, edge servers (ESs) are closer to data sources and have stronger computation and caching capabilities than terminal devices, enabling them to aggregate multi-source data within local regions. Moving the main training tasks upward to ESs can reduce terminal burden without uploading raw data and confine massive device-side interactions within local edge regions, thereby reducing cloud communication pressure and end-to-end latency.

Based on these considerations, cloud-edge-device hierarchical collaborative learning provides a more suitable solution for clients with constrained computational resources. This architecture allows clients to participate lightly, lets ESs undertake the main training tasks, and enables the cloud to coordinate cross-edge knowledge sharing~\cite{Xu2025Heterogeneous, fu2026differentially}. However, most existing hierarchical FL (HFL) methods still rely on simple parameter averaging, which is insufficient to characterize semantic relationships and update-quality differences among edge nodes~\cite{liu2020client}. In cross-modal edge data scenarios, edge nodes may cover substantially different data modalities, user behaviors, and acquisition conditions. If low-quality updates are aggregated indiscriminately, negative transfer may occur, weakening both the global model and edge models.

To address these issues, we propose Communication-Efficient FL with Quality-Aware Graph Aggregation (EdgeQGFed), a communication-efficient cloud-edge-device FL framework for cross-modal edge intelligence. EdgeQGFed shifts complex training from weak clients to ESs and uses cloud-side computation to model edge-node relationships in a quality-aware manner. The cloud builds an edge relation graph from model representations, class prototypes, and training-quality signals, and performs selective aggregation via graph attention~\cite{velivckovic2017graph}. The learned weights adapt to semantic similarity and update reliability, encouraging sharing among relevant high-quality nodes while suppressing low-quality updates. EdgeQGFed further designs resource-aware client sampling under communication budget constraints. Client selection considers data contribution, label availability, bandwidth, and dropout risk, prioritizing higher unit benefit under the communication budget. The evaluation jointly measures accuracy, communication cost, convergence efficiency, and time-to-accuracy. Experiments on public cross-modal tasks under statistical heterogeneity, label scarcity, and system heterogeneity show improved accuracy, communication efficiency, time-to-accuracy, and robustness to low-quality updates and unstable participation. The main contributions are summarized as follows:

\begin{itemize}
\item [1.] We propose EdgeQGFed, a cloud-edge-device framework for cross-modal edge intelligence, decoupling lightweight client participation, edge optimization, and cloud knowledge coordination under scarce labels and heterogeneous links.

\item [2.] We design quality-guided edge knowledge refinement, integrating cloud-guided consistency, confidence-aware pseudo-labeling, label-aware sampling, and edge summaries to improve edge updates under non-IID data.

\item [3.] We develop cloud-side quality-aware graph aggregation, which models semantic similarity and update reliability among edge nodes to enable selective knowledge sharing and personalized edge updates.

\item [4.] Extensive experiments under realistic edge-network constraints show that EdgeQGFed improves learning performance, communication efficiency, and system robustness in cross-modal edge tasks.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{section2}
\subsection{Edge-assisted and Hierarchical Federated Learning}
Conventional FL usually adopts a two-tier cloud-client architecture, where clients perform local training and upload model updates, while the cloud conducts global aggregation~\cite{mcmahan2017communication, Pei2026Adaptive}. In edge intelligence scenarios, resource-constrained devices are limited by computation, energy, bandwidth, and connection stability, making continuous complex training difficult~\cite{AbdelRaouf2026Paradigm}. Direct cloud communication by massive clients also incurs high wide-area overhead and synchronization latency~\cite{mahmoudi2025gptor}. Edge-assisted and HFL have therefore been introduced to offload part of computation and aggregation to ESs~\cite{Lin2025Hierarchical}. HierFAVG~\cite{liu2020client} performs intermediate edge aggregation to reduce client-cloud communication frequency. Wu \textit{et al}.~\cite{Wu2026Hierarchical} jointly optimize communication topology and learning direction to improve HFL stability and efficiency. Subsequent studies optimize edge association, aggregation intervals, scheduling, and resource allocation to enhance scalability~\cite{sun2026g,Trindade2026Multicriteria,li2026edge}.

Although these methods alleviate cloud communication, most still require client-side training and simplify edge or cloud aggregation as weighted averaging. Such assumptions are difficult with weak clients and unstable links, and fail to distinguish semantic relevance and update quality among edge nodes. EdgeQGFed instead shifts major training to ESs and enables selective cross-edge sharing through cloud-side quality-aware graph attention.

\subsection{Heterogeneous Federated Learning}
Real FL environments involve both data and system heterogeneity~\cite{Lin2026HASFL}. Data heterogeneity appears as non-IID distributions, label imbalance, and unequal sample sizes, while system heterogeneity appears as differences in computation, bandwidth, energy, and availability~\cite{lu2024federated, wang2025fed3scale, Chen_2026_CVPR}. FedProx~\cite{li2020federated} introduces proximal regularization to mitigate client drift. FedProto~\cite{tan2022fedproto} exchanges class prototypes for non-IID collaboration. Fed3Scale~\cite{wang2025fed3scale} employs an edge-cloud Mean Teacher architecture for semi-supervised edge training with weak clients. Federated graph clustering methods such as FedAI~\cite{han2026federated} and IFedGC~\cite{liu2026causally} handle missing attributes through inference or causal completion. These methods improve adaptability under data heterogeneity, limited labels, or missing attributes, but rarely jointly consider weak-client burden, edge-level update quality, and cloud-edge-device communication constraints.

In non-IID and cross-modal edge tasks, edge nodes may differ in pseudo-label quality, update reliability, and communication cost. Direct aggregation can introduce low-quality updates and inefficient communication. EdgeQGFed addresses these issues through resource-aware sampling, edge-side semi-supervised training, and cloud-side quality-aware graph aggregation.

\section{Preliminaries and Problem Formulation}
\label{section3}
\subsection{Cloud-Edge-Device Collaborative Learning System}
\label{section3.1}
We consider a cloud-edge-device collaborative learning system with one cloud server, ESs $\mathcal{E}=\{1,\ldots,E\}$, and client devices $\mathcal{C}=\{1,\ldots,C\}$. Each client $i$ is associated with an ES $e(i)\in\mathcal{E}$ and owns a local dataset
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{D}_i=\mathcal{D}_i^l\cup\mathcal{D}_i^u,
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{D}_i^l=\{(x,y)\}$ and $\mathcal{D}_i^u=\{x\}$ denote the labeled and unlabeled data, respectively.

To accommodate cross-modal edge tasks, a sample $x$ may be an image, sensor sequence, or network-traffic sample. We adopt a unified feature abstraction: $f_\phi(\cdot)$ denotes the encoder, $h_{\theta_e}(\cdot)$ the edge classifier, and $h_{\theta_c}(\cdot)$ the cloud teacher classifier. For images, $f_\phi$ can be a pre-trained lightweight vision encoder; for sensor sequences and traffic features, it can be an identity mapping or lightweight transform, with structured features directly handled by the edge classifier. During training, edge classifiers and the cloud teacher are mainly updated, while clients avoid complex model training.


\subsection{Communication Budget and Latency Objective}
In the $t$-th training round, the system selects a client set $\mathcal{S}_t\subseteq\mathcal{C}$ to participate in training, and the set of active ESs is denoted by $\mathcal{E}_t$. The communication budget constraint of each round is given by
\begin{equation}
    \sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_t} m_i^{\mathrm{ce}}
    + \sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_t} m_e^{\mathrm{ec}}
    + \sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_t} m_e^{\mathrm{ceg}}
    \le B_t ,
    \label{eq:budget}
\end{equation}
where $m_i^{\mathrm{ce}}$ denotes the upload volume from the client to the ES, $m_e^{\mathrm{ec}}$ denotes the upload volume from the ES to the cloud, $m_e^{\mathrm{ceg}}$ denotes the download volume from the cloud to the ES, and $B_t$ is the communication budget of the $t$-th round.

The latency of each round is modeled as
\begin{equation}
    T_t = T_t^{\mathrm{c2e}}
        + T_t^{\mathrm{edge}}
        + T_t^{\mathrm{e2c}}
        + T_t^{\mathrm{cloud}}
        + T_t^{\mathrm{c2e\_down}},
    \label{eq:latency}
\end{equation}
where $T_t^{\mathrm{c2e}}$ is the device-to-edge upload latency, $T_t^{\mathrm{edge}}$ is the edge-side training latency, $T_t^{\mathrm{e2c}}$ is the edge-to-cloud upload latency, $T_t^{\mathrm{cloud}}$ is the cloud-side aggregation latency, and $T_t^{\mathrm{c2e\_down}}$ is the cloud-to-edge download latency. In addition to final performance, we focus on time-to-target accuracy, i.e., the accumulated system latency required for the model to reach the target accuracy for the first time.

\section{EdgeQGFed Method}
\label{sec:method}
This section presents the training procedure of EdgeQGFed. Instead of allowing clients to directly train models, EdgeQGFed assigns different responsibilities to clients, ESs, and the cloud. Clients provide selected feature batches. ESs receive feature from covered clients and perform major model updates. The cloud models edge relationships from uploaded quality summaries and generates personalized edge updates and a global teacher.

In round $t$, EdgeQGFed first performs resource-aware sampling to obtain $\mathcal{S}_t$. Each active ES $e$ receives data from associated clients, learns from labeled samples, and conducts cloud-edge consistency learning and pseudo-label learning on unlabeled samples~\cite{Hu2026FedGDS, li2026towards}. After several updates, the ES uploads a summary $s_e^t$ including the model update, class prototypes, and training-quality signals. The cloud constructs a quality-aware graph, computes knowledge-sharing weights through graph attention, and sends personalized parameters back to ESs. The following subsections describe the execution order within one training round. Fig.~\ref{fig:fig1} shows the overall workflow of EdgeQGFed: client sampling, edge-side training, cloud-side graph aggregation, and personalized update dispatching, which are described in detail below.

\subsection{Resource-Aware Client Sampling}
\label{subsec:sampling}
At the beginning of each round, the participating clients need to be determined. Random selection may spend the budget on devices with low bandwidth, high dropout probability, or limited data contribution. EdgeQGFed therefore defines a resource-aware utility for client $i$ as
\begin{equation}
    U_i =
    \frac{
    \eta_l l_i + \eta_d d_i + \eta_b b_i^{\mathrm{up}} + \eta_a a_i
    }
    {(T_i^{\mathrm{up}})^{\eta_t}},
    \label{eq:client_utility}
\end{equation}
where $l_i$, $d_i$, $b_i^{\mathrm{up}}$, $a_i=1-p_i^{\mathrm{drop}}$, and $T_i^{\mathrm{up}}$ denote the labeled data ratio, normalized data size, normalized uplink bandwidth, client availability, and estimated upload latency, respectively. The coefficients $\eta_l$, $\eta_d$, $\eta_b$, $\eta_a$, and $\eta_t$ balance the contributions of data quality, data volume, bandwidth, availability, and latency. Thus, $U_i$ favors clients with informative data and stable connections while penalizing costly uploads. Since clients do not transmit raw data, the upload cost is determined by the size of the extracted feature batches, i.e., $m_i^{\mathrm{ce}}$, and the corresponding upload latency $T_i^{\mathrm{up}}$.

Clients are ranked by $U_i$, and the subset $\mathcal{S}_t$ is selected subject to edge-side parallel upload constraints and the budget in~\eqref{eq:budget}. This strategy prioritizes clients with high utility rather than simply selecting those with the largest local datasets.

\subsection{Quality-Guided Edge Knowledge Refinement}
\label{subsec:edge_training}
After client sampling, let $\mathcal{B}_e=\mathcal{B}_e^l\cup\mathcal{B}_e^u$ denote the raw-sample batches corresponding to the features uploaded by active clients of ES $e$. Each client uses a frozen pretrained encoder $f_\phi$ to map the raw input $x$ to $z=f_\phi(x)$; the encoder parameters are neither optimized locally nor aggregated globally. For clarity, we write the loss using the equivalent form $h_{\theta_e}(f_\phi(x))$. For labeled samples, with cross-entropy loss $\ell_{\mathrm{ce}}(\cdot,\cdot)$, the supervised loss is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sup}}^e =
    \frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}_e^l|}
    \sum_{(x,y)\in\mathcal{B}_e^l}
    \ell_{\mathrm{ce}}\left(h_{\theta_e}(f_\phi(x)), y\right).
    \label{eq:sup_loss}
\end{equation}
For highly imbalanced network traffic tasks such as NSL-KDD, ordinary cross-entropy tends to favor majority classes such as Normal and DoS~\cite{tavallaee2009detailed}. We therefore use a class-balanced supervised loss:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sup}}^e =
    \frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}_e^l|}
    \sum_{(x,y)\in\mathcal{B}_e^l}
    \omega_y \ell_{\mathrm{ce}}\left(h_{\theta_e}(f_\phi(x)), y\right),
    \label{eq:weighted_sup_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\omega_y$ is the weight of class $y$. Let $n_k$ denote the number of labeled samples in class $k$. The class weight is defined as
\begin{equation}
    \omega_k =
    \mathrm{clip}\left(
    \frac{
    \left(\frac{1}{K_c}\sum_{j=1}^{K_c}n_j\right)^{\alpha_w}
    }{
    (n_k+\epsilon)^{\alpha_w}
    },
    \omega_{\min}, \omega_{\max}
    \right),
    \label{eq:class_weight}
\end{equation}
where $K_c$ is the number of classes, $\alpha_w$ controls the reweighting strength, $\epsilon>0$ is a small constant, and $\omega_{\min}$ and $\omega_{\max}$ prevent the weights from becoming too small or too large. This design is enabled only for class-imbalanced tasks to improve macro-F1 and minority-class recall.

Since only a few labels are usually available at the edge side, unlabeled samples also need to be exploited. EdgeQGFed uses the cloud teacher to guide the edge student, so that local updates can benefit from cross-edge knowledge while still adapting to local data. Given a weakly augmented sample $a_w(x)$ and a strongly augmented sample $a_s(x)$, the edge model and cloud teacher output
\begin{align}
    p_e &= \mathrm{softmax}\left(h_{\theta_e}(f_\phi(a_s(x)))/T_p\right), \nonumber\\
    p_c &= \mathrm{softmax}\left(h_{\theta_c}(f_\phi(a_w(x)))/T_p\right),
    \label{eq:edge_cloud_pred}
\end{align}
respectively, where $T_p$ is the pseudo-label temperature. The edge model is expected to remain consistent under strong augmentation with the cloud teacher under weak augmentation. Therefore, the consistency loss is defined as
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{con}}^e =
    d(p_e,p_c),
    \label{eq:consistency_loss}
\end{equation}
where $d(\cdot,\cdot)$ can be the mean squared error, $\ell_1$ distance, or KL divergence. We adopt KL divergence. When predictions are unstable early, EdgeQGFed applies confidence gating to compute consistency only on reliable samples.

Using only the consistency constraint cannot provide explicit class supervision for unlabeled samples. EdgeQGFed therefore further employs confidence-weighted pseudo-labeling. Let $\hat{y}_e=\arg\max p_e$, $\hat{y}_c=\arg\max p_c$, $q_e=\max p_e$, and $q_c=\max p_c$. An unlabeled sample is used for pseudo-label training only when edge and cloud predictions agree, with its weight defined as
\begin{equation}
    w(x) =
    \mathbf{1}[\hat{y}_e=\hat{y}_c]
    \cdot
    \sigma\left(\frac{q_e-\tau_e}{\gamma}\right)
    \cdot
    \sigma\left(\frac{q_c-\tau_c}{\gamma}\right),
    \label{eq:pseudo_weight}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{1}[\cdot]$ is the indicator function, $\sigma(\cdot)$ is the sigmoid function, $\tau_e$ and $\tau_c$ are the confidence thresholds, and $\gamma$ is the smoothing temperature. The corresponding pseudo-label loss, with the same $\epsilon$ for numerical stability, is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pl}}^e =
    \frac{\sum_{x\in\mathcal{B}_e^u} w(x)
    \ell_{\mathrm{ce}}\left(h_{\theta_e}(f_\phi(a_s(x))), \hat{y}_c\right)}
    {\sum_{x\in\mathcal{B}_e^u} w(x)+\epsilon}.
    \label{eq:pseudo_loss}
\end{equation}

Finally, the training objective of ES $e$ is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_e =
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sup}}^e
    + \lambda_t \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{con}}^e
    + \lambda_p \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pl}}^e,
    \label{eq:edge_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_t$ is gradually increased through warm-up, and $\lambda_p$ controls the pseudo-label loss. For the five-class network-traffic classification task, pseudo-labeling is activated later and uses higher thresholds to prevent high-confidence pseudo-labels of majority classes from prematurely suppressing minority classes such as R2L and U2R.

\subsection{Handling Label Scarcity and Class Imbalance}
\label{subsec:stratified_label}
In label-scarce and non-IID settings, random labeling may spend the limited annotation budget on locally dominant classes, leaving minority classes without supervised anchors. This weakens class prototype construction and may cause pseudo-labeling to reinforce majority-class predictions. EdgeQGFed therefore adopts stratified label sampling for class-imbalanced tasks. For the local index set $\mathcal{I}_i$ of client $i$, its labeling budget is
\begin{equation}
    L_i=\min\left(|\mathcal{I}_i|,\max(\lfloor r|\mathcal{I}_i|\rfloor,L_{\min})\right),
    \label{eq:label_budget}
\end{equation}
where $r$ is the label ratio and $L_{\min}$ is the minimum number of labeled samples for each client. The system first preserves representative labeled samples from locally visible classes and then fills the remaining budget with randomly selected samples. This strategy does not change the overall label ratio, but provides more reliable anchors for supervised learning, prototype estimation, and pseudo-label calibration.

% For NSL-KDD five-class classification, a portion of labeled samples is retained in each training batch and resampled by class weights, so that minority classes such as R2L and U2R appear more frequently in supervised training. This affects only training and leaves the test distribution unchanged. Therefore, macro-F1, macro recall, and per-class recall are more suitable metrics.

\subsection{Edge Summary Construction}
\label{subsec:summary}
After local updates, the ES does not upload raw data to the cloud. Instead, it uploads an edge summary that reflects model state and training quality:
\begin{equation}
    s_e^t = \left(\theta_e^t, P_e^t, n_e^t, \rho_e^t, r_e^t, c_e^t, N_e^t\right),
    \label{eq:summary}
\end{equation}
where $\theta_e^t$, $P_e^t$, $n_e^t$, $\rho_e^t$, $r_e^t$, $c_e^t$, and $N_e^t$ denote the uploaded edge classifier, class prototype matrix, class-wise prototype counts, average pseudo-label weight, labeled-sample ratio, average prediction confidence, and number of processed samples, respectively. Together, these quantities characterize the update direction, class semantics, and reliability of ES $e$. For clarity, the superscript $t$ is omitted in the following graph aggregation formulas when no ambiguity arises.

Class prototypes characterize the data semantics observed by an edge node. For class $k$, the prototype is defined as
\begin{equation}
    P_e^{t,k} =
    \frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}_{e,k}^l|}
    \sum_{(x,y)\in\mathcal{B}_{e,k}^l} f_\phi(x),
    \label{eq:prototype}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{B}_{e,k}^l$ denotes labeled class-$k$ samples in the current batch of ES $e$. If a class is absent, its prototype count is zero, and its contribution is reduced during cloud aggregation. By uploading summaries instead of raw data, EdgeQGFed reduces communication and privacy overhead while preserving information for cross-edge coordination.

\subsection{Cloud Knowledge Coordination }
\label{subsec:graph_aggregation}
\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Cloud-Side Quality-Aware Graph Attention Aggregation}
\label{alg:graph_aggregation}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Active edge summaries $\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{edge}}_t=\{s_e^t\}_{e\in\mathcal{E}_t}$, graph temperature $\tau_g$, coefficients $\alpha_p,\alpha_q,\alpha_r,\alpha_c$, self-connection bias $\beta$;
\ENSURE Cloud teacher model $\theta_c$, personalized edge parameters $\{\tilde{\theta}_e\}_{e\in\mathcal{E}_t}$;
\FOR{each active edge $e\in\mathcal{E}_t$}
    \STATE Extract the model signature $u_e$ and prototype signature $v_e$;
\ENDFOR
\FOR{each pair of active edges $(i,j)$}
    \STATE Compute the quality-aware score $a_{ij}$ according to Eq.~\eqref{eq:attention_score};
\ENDFOR
\STATE Obtain the attention matrix $A$ according to Eq.~\eqref{eq:attention_matrix};
\FOR{each target edge $i\in\mathcal{E}_t$}
    \STATE Generate personalized parameters $\tilde{\theta}_i$ according to Eq.~\eqref{eq:personalized_param};
\ENDFOR
\STATE Update the cloud teacher model according to Eqs.~\eqref{eq:global_param} and~\eqref{eq:ema};
\STATE Send personalized parameters or global fallback parameters to ESs;
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

After receiving edge summaries, the cloud identifies which nodes should share knowledge. Instead of averaging all updates indiscriminately, EdgeQGFed models active ESs as graph nodes and builds a quality-aware graph that accounts for data distribution, label quality, and pseudo-label reliability, thereby avoiding unreliable knowledge propagation.

For edge node $e$, the cloud extracts a model signature $u_e$ from model parameters and a prototype signature $v_e$ from class prototypes. For active edge nodes $i$ and $j$, the attention score is defined as
\begin{align}
    &\ a_{ij} =
     \mathrm{cos}(u_i,u_j)
    + \alpha_p \mathrm{cos}(v_i,v_j)
    + \alpha_q (1-|\rho_i-\rho_j|) \nonumber \\
    &+ \alpha_r (1-|r_i-r_j|)
    + \alpha_c (1-|c_i-c_j|)
    + \beta \mathbf{1}[i=j],
    \label{eq:attention_score}
\end{align}
where model and prototype signatures characterize semantic similarity, while $\rho_i$, $r_i$, and $c_i$ reflect pseudo-label quality, label ratio, and prediction confidence. Thus, two edges exchange more knowledge when their semantics are close and their training signals are similarly reliable. The coefficients $\alpha_p$, $\alpha_q$, $\alpha_r$, and $\alpha_c$ control these quality signals, and $\beta$ is a self-connection bias preserving edge-local knowledge.

The cloud then normalizes the attention scores to obtain the graph attention matrix:
\begin{equation}
    A_{ij} =
    \frac{\exp(a_{ij}/\tau_g)}
    {\sum_{k\in\mathcal{E}_t}\exp(a_{ik}/\tau_g)},
    \label{eq:attention_matrix}
\end{equation}
where $\tau_g$ is the graph attention temperature. A larger $A_{ij}$ indicates that edge node $i$ should absorb more knowledge from edge node $j$ when generating its personalized model. The cloud then generates personalized parameters for each target edge node as
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{\theta}_i =
    \sum_{j\in\mathcal{E}_t} A_{ij}\theta_j.
    \label{eq:personalized_param}
\end{equation}

In addition to personalized edge parameters, the cloud maintains a global teacher model. EdgeQGFed computes global aggregation weights according to the number of samples processed by each edge node and the pseudo-label quality:
\begin{equation}
    \bar{\theta} =
    \sum_{i\in\mathcal{E}_t}
    \omega_i \tilde{\theta}_i,
    \quad
    \omega_i =
    \frac{N_i(\rho_i+1)}
    {\sum_{j\in\mathcal{E}_t} N_j(\rho_j+1)}.
    \label{eq:global_param}
\end{equation}
If AEMA is adopted, the cloud teacher model is updated by
\begin{equation}
    \theta_c \leftarrow
    \mu \theta_c + (1-\mu)\bar{\theta},
    \label{eq:ema}
\end{equation}
where $\mu$ is the smoothing coefficient. If FedAvg~\cite{mcmahan2017communication} is adopted, $\bar{\theta}$ directly replaces the cloud teacher model. Finally, the cloud sends $\tilde{\theta}_i$ to the corresponding ES. For edge nodes without personalized parameters in the current round, the cloud sends global fallback parameters to avoid long-term staleness. Algorithm~\ref{alg:graph_aggregation} summarizes the cloud-side quality-aware graph aggregation procedure.

\subsection{Overall Training Procedure}
\label{subsec:procedure}
Combining the above modules, one EdgeQGFed round includes client selection, edge training, summary uploading, cloud graph aggregation, and update dispatching. Algorithm~\ref{alg:edgeqgfed} gives the complete cloud-edge-client training procedure. It first selects clients by resource-aware utility, then each active edge constructs labeled and unlabeled batches and updates its model with supervised, consistency, and pseudo-label losses. When the cloud aggregation interval is reached, edges upload compact summaries instead of raw data; the cloud invokes Algorithm~\ref{alg:graph_aggregation} to update the teacher model and dispatch personalized parameters. This highlights EdgeQGFed’s role separation: clients participate lightly, edges optimize, and the cloud coordinates cross-edge knowledge sharing.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Cloud-Edge-Client Collaborative Training Procedure of EdgeQGFed}
\label{alg:edgeqgfed}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Client set $\mathcal{C}$, ES set $\mathcal{E}$, cloud teacher \\model $\theta_c$, edge models $\{\theta_e\}_{e\in\mathcal{E}}$, communication \\budget $B_t$, total number of training rounds $T$;
\ENSURE Cloud teacher model $\theta_c$, edge models $\{\theta_e\}_{e\in\mathcal{E}}$
\FOR{$t=1$ to $T$}
    \STATE Perform resource-aware client sampling according \\to Eq.~\eqref{eq:client_utility} to obtain $\mathcal{S}_t$;
    \FOR{each active ES $e\in\mathcal{E}_t$}
        \STATE Receive feature batches from associated clients, and construct $\mathcal{B}_e^l$ and $\mathcal{B}_e^u$;
        \STATE Compute the supervised loss according to Eq.~\eqref{eq:sup_loss} \\or Eq.~\eqref{eq:weighted_sup_loss};
        \STATE Compute the unlabeled losses according \\to Eq.~\eqref{eq:consistency_loss} and Eq.~\eqref{eq:pseudo_loss};
        \STATE Update the edge model $\theta_e$ according to Eq.~\eqref{eq:edge_loss};
        \IF{the cloud aggregation interval is reached}
            \STATE Construct and upload the edge summary $s_e^t$ according to Eq.~\eqref{eq:summary};
        \ENDIF
    \ENDFOR
    \IF{the cloud receives edge summaries}
        \STATE Invoke Algorithm~\ref{alg:graph_aggregation} to perform quality-aware \\graph attention aggregation;
        \STATE Update the cloud teacher model and dispatch personalized edge parameters;
    \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Convergence Analysis}
\label{subsec:convergence}
Since resource-aware sampling changes the active edge set $\mathcal{E}_t$ and aggregation weights $\omega^t=\{\omega_e^t\}$ across rounds, we analyze a fixed global objective rather than a dynamic one:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{F}(\theta)=\sum_{e=1}^{E}\pi_e\mathcal{F}_e(\theta),
    \quad \pi_e\ge0,\quad \sum_{e=1}^{E}\pi_e=1 .
    \label{eq:fixed_global_objective}
\end{equation}
Here, $\mathcal{F}_e$ denotes the ideal risk of edge $e$, and the supervised, consistency, and pseudo-label training terms in Eq.~\eqref{eq:edge_loss} are regarded as a biased stochastic gradient estimate of $\mathcal{F}_e$~\cite{mcmahan2017communication, li2020federated}.

\begin{assumption}[Standard Conditions]
Each $\mathcal{F}_e$ is $L$-smooth, and $\mathcal{F}$ is lower bounded by $\mathcal{F}_\star$. The local stochastic gradient $g_e^t$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb{E}\|g_e^t-\mathbb{E}[g_e^t|\theta_e^t]\|^2\le\sigma^2,\quad
    \|\mathbb{E}[g_e^t|\theta_e^t]-\nabla\mathcal{F}_e(\theta_e^t)\|\le\delta_t .
\end{equation}
The edge heterogeneity and resource-sampling bias satisfy
\begin{align}
    &\sum_{e=1}^{E}\pi_e
    \|\nabla\mathcal{F}_e(\theta)-\nabla\mathcal{F}(\theta)\|^2
    \le \zeta^2,
    \label{eq:heterogeneity_bound}\\
    &
    \left\|\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{E}_t,\omega^t}\!\left[
    \sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_t}
    \omega_e^t\nabla\mathcal{F}_e(\theta)\right]
    -\nabla\mathcal{F}(\theta)\right\|
    \le \epsilon_{s,t}.
    \label{eq:sampling_bias_bound}
\end{align}
If resource-aware sampling is unbiased, then $\epsilon_{s,t}=0$. The cloud-side attention matrix $A^t$ at round $t$ satisfies $A^t\ge0$ and $A^t\mathbf{1}=\mathbf{1}$, and is non-expansive under the adopted norm.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Non-Convex Convergence of EdgeQGFed]
Suppose Assumption 1 holds. Let each active edge perform at most $K$ steps of local SGD in each round~\cite{stich2018local}, and let the learning rate satisfy $\eta\le 1/(c_0LK)$, where $c_0>0$ is a sufficiently large constant. Let $\bar{\theta}^t$ be the cloud-aggregated model in round $t$. Then there exist constants $C_1,C_2,C_3,C_4>0$ independent of $T$ such that
\begin{align}
    \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}
    \mathbb{E}\|\nabla\mathcal{F}(\bar{\theta}^t)\|^2
    \le&
    \frac{2(\mathcal{F}(\bar{\theta}^0)-\mathcal{F}_\star)}{\eta KT}
    +C_1\eta\sigma^2 \nonumber\\
    &+C_2\eta^2K^2\zeta^2
    +C_3\bar{\delta}^2
    +C_4\bar{\epsilon}_s^2 ,
    \label{eq:convergence_bound}
\end{align}
where
$\bar{\delta}^2=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\delta_t^2$ and
$\bar{\epsilon}_s^2=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\epsilon_{s,t}^2$.
When $K=\mathcal{O}(1)$ and $\eta=\mathcal{O}(T^{-1/2})$, the upper bound of the average gradient norm becomes
$\mathcal{O}(T^{-1/2})+\mathcal{O}(T^{-1}\zeta^2)+
\mathcal{O}(\bar{\delta}^2)+\mathcal{O}(\bar{\epsilon}_s^2)$.
\end{theorem}

\textbf{Proof Sketch.}
By $L$-smoothness, the biased stochastic gradient descent lemma, and Young's inequality, pseudo-label bias introduces a term of order $C_0\eta\delta_t^2$, while stochastic gradient noise introduces a term of order $C_0\eta^2\sigma^2$~\cite{bottou2018optimization}. Accumulating the single-step descent over $K$ local steps and combining edge heterogeneity and resource-sampling bias yield
\begin{align}
    \mathbb{E}\mathcal{F}(\bar{\theta}^{t+1})
    \le&
    \mathcal{F}(\bar{\theta}^{t})
    -c_1\eta K
    \mathbb{E}\|\nabla\mathcal{F}(\bar{\theta}^t)\|^2
    +C_0\eta^2K\sigma^2 \nonumber\\
    &+C_0\eta K(\delta_t^2+\epsilon_{s,t}^2)+C_0\eta^3K^3\zeta^2 .
    \label{eq:round_descent_fixed}
\end{align}
Here, $c_1, C_0>0$ are constants. The non-expansiveness of the attention matrix ensures that graph aggregation does not amplify the above error terms. Summing Eq.~\eqref{eq:round_descent_fixed} over $t = \{0, \ldots, T-1\}$, using $\mathcal{F}(\theta)\ge\mathcal{F}_\star$, and dividing both sides by $\eta KT$ lead to Eq.~\eqref{eq:convergence_bound}. Setting $K=\mathcal{O}(1)$ and $\eta=\mathcal{O}(T^{-1/2})$ gives the asymptotic conclusion. \hfill$\square$

The above result shows that EdgeQGFed can converge to a neighborhood of a first-order stationary point when the pseudo-label bias and resource-sampling bias are controllable. This neighborhood is jointly determined by stochastic gradient noise, edge heterogeneity, pseudo-label bias, and sampling bias. If resource-aware sampling is unbiased, $\bar{\epsilon}_s=0$; if further $\bar{\delta}\to0$, the standard $\mathcal{O}(T^{-1/2})$ non-convex convergence behavior can be recovered.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{subsec:exp_setup}
\textbf{Datasets and Tasks.}
We use four public datasets covering image, sensor-sequence, and network-traffic tasks. As shown in Table~\ref{tab:datasets}, CIFAR-10/100 support image classification, HARBox evaluates user-level sensor-sequence recognition, and NSL-KDD evaluates network-traffic recognition~\cite{krizhevsky2009learning, ouyang2022clusterfl, tavallaee2009detailed}. For NSL-KDD, continuous features are normalized and discrete fields are numerically encoded. Since the original binary task reaches nearly $99\%$ accuracy, we use a harder five-class setting: Normal, DoS, Probe, R2L, and U2R, and evaluate personalization under client-level non-IID traffic distributions.

\textbf{System Configuration.}
The default system includes one cloud server, multiple ESs, and clients. Image tasks use 4 ESs and 128 clients, while HARBox and NSL-KDD use 5 ESs and 100 clients. In each round, active clients upload feature batches to associated ESs for training, and the cloud coordinates cross-edge aggregation. Heterogeneous edge links are used by default, with communication budget, bandwidth, and dropout rate varied in system experiments.

\textbf{Implementation Environment.}
All experiments run on a workstation with an NVIDIA A100 GPU with 40 GB memory and an Intel(R) Xeon(R) Gold 6330 CPU @ 2.00 GHz.

\section{Conclusion}
This paper proposes EdgeQGFed, a communication-efficient cloud-edge-device collaborative learning framework for cross-modal edge intelligence. It shifts the main training workload to ESs, allowing resource-constrained clients to participate through lightweight data contribution. At the cloud layer, quality-aware graph attention models semantic correlations and update reliability among edge nodes, enabling selective knowledge sharing beyond uniform averaging. To fit realistic edge networks, EdgeQGFed integrates resource-aware client sampling and edge-side semi-supervised learning, thereby improving model performance and training efficiency under communication constraints, link heterogeneity, and unstable participation. Experiments on image, sensor-sequence, and network-traffic tasks demonstrate consistent gains in learning performance, network efficiency, and robustness. Future work will study asynchronous collaborative training over larger edge topologies and adaptive model deployment for more complex edge tasks.
